1. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
--------------------

Scientific question
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

The project studies loop-erased random walks (LERWs) in random environments.
Its primary question is whether random site disorder changes winding-variance
growth from ordinary logarithmic roughness,

    Var(W_L) approximately C log L,

to a super-rough regime,

    Var(W_L) approximately C (log L)^2.

It estimates an effective exponent p using

    Var(W_L) = C (log L)^p,
    log Var(W_L) = log C + p log log L.

Thus p=1 corresponds to ordinary logarithmic growth and p=2 to squared-log
growth. It also directly compares the additive finite-size models

    a + b log L
    a + b (log L)^2.

Two completed production experiments
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

A. Strict-annealed single-walk experiment

    - 3,790 completed atomic tasks.
    - 379,000 LERWs.
    - 379,000 independently seeded environments.
    - Exactly one walk in every environment.
    - 15 weight specifications.
    - L=16,32,64,128,256,512,1024,2048,4096 for every specification.
    - Baseline and Gamma(shape=1) also have L=8192.
    - Zero failed rows and no reused environment seed.
    - Effective p values: 1.011 to 1.160.
    - All 95% bootstrap intervals are far below p=2.
    - Ordinary log has lower BIC in 14/15 cases.
    - Pareto(alpha=2) is an effective BIC tie, Delta BIC=-0.24.

B. Paired “double-dimer” winding-difference experiment

    - 3,790 completed atomic tasks.
    - 379,000 independently seeded environments.
    - Exactly two conditionally independent LERWs in each shared environment.
    - 758,000 raw walks.
    - 379,000 paired observables Delta W = W_1 - W_2.
    - Same 15 specifications and size/replication design as experiment A.
    - Zero failed rows and no incomplete tasks.
    - Effective p values: 1.036 to 1.187 for Var(W_1-W_2).
    - All 95% pair-bootstrap intervals are far below p=2.
    - Ordinary log has lower BIC in 12/15 cases.
    - Squared-log additive fits have lower BIC for Pareto(alpha=3) and
      Lognormal(sigma=1); Lognormal(sigma=0.5) is effectively tied.
    - Those three effective p estimates are still only 1.187, 1.166, and 1.070,
      so the local BIC preferences are finite-size curvature, not robust p=2
      evidence.

Overall conclusion
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

Both observables give the same broad answer: over the simulated sizes there is
no robust (log L)^2 winding-variance regime. Effective exponents stay close to
one and every bootstrap interval remains far below two. Some intervals exclude
p=1 modestly on the high side, so “finite-size effective exponent” is the right
language; the data are not proof of exactly p=1 asymptotics either.


2. EXACT MATHEMATICAL MODEL
---------------------------

Domain and stopping rule
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

For integer L>=1, the walk starts at (0,0) inside

    D_L = {(x,y) in Z^2 : |x|<L and |y|<L}

and stops on the first hit of

    boundary D_L = {(x,y) in Z^2 : |x|=L or |y|=L}.

The walk is nearest-neighbour and therefore hits this boundary exactly.

Random environment
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

At each site x, the environment has four positive outgoing directional
weights:

    (w_x,N, w_x,E, w_x,S, w_x,W).

The four weights at a site are independent draws from the selected weight
family, and sites are independent in the deterministic pseudorandom
construction. Conditional on the environment,

    P(X_(n+1)=x+e_d | X_n=x, environment)
      = w_x,d / sum_{d' in {N,E,S,W}} w_x,d'.

Critical distinction: generally w_x,E is not equal to w_(x+E),W. This is a
directed, generally nonreversible site environment, not an undirected
random-conductance model. Historical parser aliases ending in “_edges” still
map to this site-i.i.d. generator; they do not create shared edge weights.

Loop erasure
~~~~~~~~~~~~

Chronological loop erasure is maintained online. The simulator stores a
self-avoiding path plus a map from retained sites to path indices. A new site is
appended. If a retained site is revisited, all path sites after its prior index
are deleted and the path is truncated. The full raw random-walk trajectory is
not stored.

Winding convention
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

LERW edge directions are encoded E=0, N=1, W=2, S=3 modulo 4. For each pair of
consecutive path edges:

    left quarter-turn  -> +1
    right quarter-turn -> -1
    straight           ->  0

An immediate U-turn is rejected as inconsistent with a self-avoiding
nearest-neighbour path. W_L is the number of left quarter-turns minus right
quarter-turns. It is not stored in radians. Angular winding is

    Theta_L = (pi/2) W_L,
    Var(Theta_L) = (pi^2/4) Var(W_L).

That conversion changes amplitudes but not exponent p.

Sampling meanings
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

Annealed variance pools environment and walk randomness:

    V_A(L) = Var_(environment,walk)(W_L).

With repeated walks in an environment, the general code can also estimate

    V_Q(L) = E_environment[Var_walk(W_L | environment)].

The strict production experiment has one walk per environment, so quenched
variance is not identifiable and is deliberately excluded from strict fits.

The paired experiment samples one environment and then two independent walk RNG
streams within it. Its observable is

    Delta W = W_1 - W_2.

The important variance identity is

    Var(W_1-W_2)
      = Var(W_1) + Var(W_2) - 2 Cov(W_1,W_2).

Production data satisfy this identity numerically with maximum absolute
residual about 1.51e-14.


3. WEIGHT DISTRIBUTIONS
-----------------------

Every non-baseline site gets four independent draws, one per outgoing
direction. The implemented production families are normalized to mean one
where a mean exists:

    baseline
        w = 1 deterministically.

    exponential
        w ~ Exponential(mean=1).

    gamma, shape k>0
        w ~ Gamma(shape=k, scale=1/k).
        Production k: 0.5, 1.0, 2.0.

    lognormal, sigma>0
        log w ~ Normal(-sigma^2/2, sigma^2).
        Production sigma: 0.5, 1.0.

    pareto, alpha>1
        shape alpha, scale (alpha-1)/alpha.
        Production alpha: 2.0, 3.0.

    uniform, 0<a<1
        w ~ Uniform(1-a, 1+a).
        Production a: 0.7.

    beta, a>0
        w = 2B where B ~ Beta(a,a).
        Production a: 0.5.

    weibull, shape k>0
        scale = 1/Gamma(1+1/k).
        Production k: 0.7.

    inverse_gamma, alpha>1
        shape alpha, scale alpha-1.
        Production alpha: 2.2.

    bernoulli, 0<a<1
        w is 1-a or 1+a with probability 1/2 each.
        Production a: 0.8.

    triangular, 0<a<1
        symmetric triangular distribution on [1-a,1+a], mode 1.
        Production a: 0.8.

Accepted aliases include baseline/symmetric, exponential/exp/exp_edges, and
parameterized names ending in “_edges”. Again, “_edges” does not change the
underlying site-directed model.

6. IMPLEMENTATION AND REPRODUCIBILITY DETAILS
---------------------------------------------

Coordinates and cache
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

Each Int32 (x,y) coordinate is packed into one UInt64 key. Disordered
environments use a bounded, power-of-two, direct-mapped cache of four-tuples of
weights. Cache capacity is approximately L^2/4, clamped from 2^10 to 2^23.
Baseline uses capacity 1.

Cache collisions/evictions are scientifically harmless because site weights are
regenerated deterministically from the environment seed, canonical model,
parameter, and packed coordinate. sampled_site_count in raw output is occupied
cache slots, not the number of distinct sites ever visited.

Seeds and RNGs
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

stable_seed joins its arguments with colons, hashes the string with SHA-256, and
interprets the first eight digest bytes as a platform-independent UInt64.
Canonical parameter JSON has sorted keys. Task/environment/walk seeds include
the base seed and relevant identifiers. Scientific results therefore do not
depend on thread scheduling.

Site weights use a coordinate-addressed Random.Xoshiro stream seeded through a
SplitMix64 transform. Walk transition choices use StableRNGs.StableRNG with a
separate deterministic walk seed.

Source fingerprint
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

Every raw row stores:

    short Git commit + source.<12-hex SHA prefix>

The hash covers all active Julia source files plus Project.toml and
Manifest.toml. It captures dirty source content even though the Git short hash
remains the last commit.

Strict rows were produced by:

    2f619b8+source.020484b0bc41

Double rows and the current source state use:

    2f619b8+source.e0a560f34bd4

Both campaigns record Julia 1.12.6 and schema batch_v6_julia.

Threading and task order
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

run_batch uses Threads.@threads across environments. Each environment has its
own vector, and results are concatenated in environment order. Tasks are run
sequentially by run_campaign while each task uses the available Julia threads.

Walk cap
~~~~~~~~

Default maximum raw steps:

    max(100_000, 2_000 * L^2).

A CLI --max-steps override exists. A walk exceeding the cap becomes an explicit
failed result row.

Restart safety
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

A batch is complete only if its CSV:

    - exists,
    - has exactly num_environments * walks_per_environment rows, and
    - has status “ok” in every row.

Complete files are skipped. An incomplete existing file stops the campaign
unless --rerun-failed is supplied. Writes go to a hidden temporary CSV in the
target directory and are then atomically moved to the final name. --force is
available for the single-task runner.

Result paths
~~~~~~~~~~~~

    <output>/site_iid/<safe-distribution>/L_dddd/batch_dddd.csv

Decimal points become “p” in safe distribution directory names, for example:

    gamma_shape_1
    pareto_alpha_2
    triangular_a_0p8

--------------------------------------

Common validation
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

For every task in the frozen config, the analyzer records path existence,
expected rows, observed rows, successful rows, failed rows, and completeness.
It rejects incomplete campaigns by default. --allow-incomplete exists but must
not be used for final production claims.

The analyzer rejects duplicate keys:

    (distribution, parameters, L, batch_id, environment_id, walk_id).

Strict-specific validation
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

    - walks_per_environment must be 1 in the config.
    - every raw row must have walk_id=0.
    - every environment seed must be globally unique.

    3,790/3,790 tasks complete
    379,000/379,000 rows successful
    0 failed
    379,000 unique environment seeds

Double-specific validation
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

    - walks_per_environment must be 2 in the config.
    - every environment group must contain exactly two successful rows.
    - sorted walk IDs must be {0,1}.
    - both walks must share one environment seed.
    - the two walk seeds must differ.
    - environment seeds must be unique across pairs.
    - walk seeds must be globally unique.

Current result:

    3,790/3,790 tasks complete
    758,000/758,000 raw walks successful
    0 failed
    379,000 validated pairs

Cell summaries
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

Rows are grouped by distribution, parameter JSON, and L. Variance uses the
corrected sample variance. The summary also stores counts, mean winding, exit
coordinates divided by L, mean raw length, mean LERW length, and mean runtime.

For double data, summary.csv additionally contains W_1 and W_2 means/variances,
their covariance/correlation, pooled single-winding variance, difference
variance, variance ratio, and the variance-identity residual.

Variance standard errors
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

The annealed/difference variance SE is an environment leave-one-cluster-out
jackknife. In strict data a cluster has one walk. In double data one pair is the
analysis observation for one environment.

Power fit
~~~~~~~~~

For each distribution specification, the code performs unweighted ordinary
least squares across 9 sizes, or 10 for baseline and Gamma(1):

    x_L = log log L
    y_L = log estimated variance
    y_L = intercept + p x_L + error.

The table stores p, its OLS SE and normal interval, R^2, SSE, AIC, and BIC.

Bootstrap interval
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

Production analyses use 1,000 percentile bootstrap replicates. Environments are
resampled with replacement inside every (distribution,L) cell. All walks in a
sampled environment remain together. The slope is refit each replicate; the
2.5% and 97.5% quantiles give the reported interval.

Additive model comparison
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

Unweighted OLS compares:

    variance = a + b log L
    variance = a + b (log L)^2.

With n size points and two fitted parameters:

    AIC = n log(SSE/n) + 4
    BIC = n log(SSE/n) + 2 log n

The reported difference is:

    Delta BIC = BIC(log^2) - BIC(log).

Positive Delta BIC favors ordinary log. Negative favors squared log. With only
9 or 10 size-level residuals, values near zero are descriptive ties.

Other diagnostics
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

    pointwise_ratios.csv
        Variance, log variance, log log L, and log variance/log log L.

    local_effective_exponents.csv
        Adjacent-size slope
        [log V(L_2)-log V(L_1)]/[log log L_2-log log L_1]
        at geometric midpoint sqrt(L_1 L_2). These are noisy diagnostics.

specifications. Var(W_1-W_2) divided by twice the pooled single-winding
variance ranges from 0.828 to 1.083. Sharing an environment therefore creates
modest case-dependent covariance, but no systematic shift toward p=2.

Relative to strict single-walk p, seven double estimates increase and eight
decrease. Largest increase: Pareto(3), 1.011 to 1.187. Largest decrease:
Gamma(1), 1.146 to 1.036.

13. SCIENTIFIC AND STATISTICAL LIMITATIONS
------------------------------------------

Model-identification limitations
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

    - Directed outgoing weights are not undirected conductances.
    - “_edges” aliases do not implement shared edge disorder.
    - The domain is a square with half-side/boundary coordinate L.
    - Winding is a signed quarter-turn count, not radians.
    - A formal mapping from this LERW observable to any dimer-height observable
      is not in the repository.

Finite-size/statistical limitations
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

    - Fits are unweighted across L even though cell uncertainty and replication
      vary considerably, especially at L=2048,4096,8192.
    - Both the power model and two additive models omit corrections to scaling.
    - BIC uses only 9 or 10 size points and a common Gaussian residual model.
    - Near-zero BIC differences are ties, not discoveries.
    - Bootstrap intervals use 1,000 percentile replicates; endpoint Monte Carlo
      error is not separately reported.
    - Fifteen specifications are compared without formal multiple-testing
      adjustment.
    - Strict data have one walk per environment and cannot estimate quenched
      within-environment variance.
    - Double data have one pair per environment; covariance is estimated across
      environments at each cell, not through repeated pairs within an
      environment.
    - Normal plot error bars use jackknife SEs, while forest-plot exponent
      intervals use bootstrap quantiles. Label them distinctly.
    - Local effective exponents are noisy and should remain secondary.

Implementation/audit limitations
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

    - sampled_site_count is cache occupancy, not total unique visited sites.
    - The source fingerprint includes a source hash but not a stored patch.
    - 64-bit seeds are empirically unique in production, not collision-proof for
      arbitrary future campaigns.
    - Default step-cap arithmetic was designed for current sizes, not arbitrary
      L near the Int32 coordinate limit.
    - Tests establish internal consistency, not equivalence to an external
      mathematical/physics model.

This is a Julia 1.12.6 Monte Carlo project for winding of loop-erased random
walks in directed site-i.i.d. random transition environments. Two validated

379,000 unique environments, and (2) 758,000 raw walks arranged as 379,000
