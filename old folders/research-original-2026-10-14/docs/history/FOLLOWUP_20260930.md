## 1. Diagnose CFTP coalescence

The diagnostic regenerated all 240 pilot environments from their recorded
parameter-aware seeds. Environment and replica seed identities were checked
against the downloaded status table. It did not need renewed Hamilton access.
For all 80 random environments, it computed stable log odds and minority
heat-bath probabilities; the two deterministic control families were audited
as well.

Among 30,160 random-model faces, 2,809 have minority probabilities below
1/65,536. The smallest edge weight is 3.66e-33 and the smallest minority
probability is 3.86e-33. Nine horizontal probabilities, in six environments,
round to exactly one in the existing Float64 implementation. The controls
have minority probabilities 4/9 and 1/2 and no such rounding.

This exposes a numerical limitation: the mathematical perfect-sampling theorem
does not remove floating-point transition error. Exact coalescence of the
implemented maps alone does not prove exact sampling of the ideal real-valued
weighted law. Neither clipping weights nor suppressing rare transitions is a
valid silent repair, since both change the model. A new numeric kernel would
need a versioned stream identity, rare-event validation and regression checks;
old checkpoints cannot silently resume under changed random maps.

For the first four side-8 random environments, both original replica streams
were replayed from true extrema at horizons 1, 16, 256, 4,096, 65,536 and
262,144. The reference and accelerated maps agreed at the first horizon. None
of these eight streams coalesced at the larger cap. Median disagreeing-face
counts fell from 49 to 25.5 at 65,536 and 16.5 at 262,144. The final maximum
height gaps are 4 or 8. Each final replay attempted 12,845,056 updates per bound
and still made hundreds of thousands of actual changes: the chains are not
simply frozen everywhere.

None of these four environments has a probability rounded to one. Thus the
observed slow coupling is not explained solely by that numerical issue. The
results are consistent with severe disorder-related bottlenecks, but do not
identify a unique mechanism or measure the actual mixing time. CFTP
coalescence can be slower than equilibrium relaxation. Simply increasing the
budget by a factor of four did not resolve these cases.

Evidence: `weight_diagnostics.csv`, `cftp_replay.csv`, and
`cftp_endpoint_maps.csv` in the dated validation directory. Diagnostic endpoint
maps are not additional certified samples.

## 2. Analyse the recovered September probes

All 23,799 completed pairs were recovered; the original planned 23,800th pair
remains unresolved. This is the all-edge mean-one Gamma(1) experiment, not the
new two-family Gamma(0.2)/Gamma(0.25) law.

The analysis uses 10,000 whole-environment bootstrap resamples within each
size/model. Both replicas, centre heights, original rightward increments and
all recovered probes move together. It reports thermal, disorder and total
moments with pointwise percentile intervals. The variance identity is checked
against explicit resampling in tests. Intervals describe the observed sample;
they do not account for the unresolved environment or establish simultaneous
coverage over all fitted windows.

The principal illustrative diagonal probe has total displacement r=L/4
(with recorded integer rounding). Fits below use disorder covariance against
an intercept, log(distance), and squared log(distance).

| Observable | Sizes | Squared-log coefficient | Pointwise 95% interval |
|---|---|---:|---:|
| Symmetric diagonal, rho=1/4 | 8–32 | 0.9955 | [0.4093, 1.6003] |
| Symmetric diagonal, rho=1/4 | 12–32 | -0.3801 | [-1.6387, 0.9152] |
| Symmetric diagonal, rho=1/4 | 16–32 | -3.2509 | [-6.0980, -0.3325] |
| Symmetric horizontal, rho=1/4 | 8–32 | 0.2402 | [-0.2999, 0.7753] |
| Original rightward, rho=1/4 | 8–32 | -0.4113 | [-0.9320, 0.1076] |
| Original centre height | 8–32 | 0.0446 | [-0.3311, 0.4218] |

The diagonal estimate is positive over the full window but reverses sign when
small sizes are removed. This is strong cutoff sensitivity, not robust evidence
for an asymptotic positive squared-log term. The centre point estimate reproduces
the earlier report. The paired symmetric-horizontal minus rightward curvature
contrast is 0.6516 with interval [-0.0735, 1.3774], so this comparison does not
establish a difference at that interval level. All fitted uniform-control
curvature intervals contain zero; this does not prove the sampler correct.

The planned pooled rho=1/8 and rho=1/4 fits were refused: small-size duplicate
probe removal and rounded separations leave insufficient distinct physical
distances. Eighteen unsuitable designs were explicitly recorded rather than
silently filling absent probes or treating repeated distances as new scales.
There are 152 moment rows and 24 accepted exploratory fits. No result is a
matched Aztec–square law contrast.

Evidence: `recovered_intervals.csv` and `recovered_analysis.json`.

## 3. Reassess the corrected Aztec benchmark

The corrected orientation adapter generated 64 paired environments at each
order 16, 32, 48, 64, 96 and 128: 384 environments, with weights and both tilings
saved. Master seed 2026093002 is distinct from previous pilots. The same
Gamma(0.2)/Gamma(0.25), scale-one law and diagonal unit-height probes were used.
The mathematical method is weighted shuffling, implemented in floating point;
the small exact-distribution audits validate the orientation correction on
their enumerated test cases, not an arbitrary-precision guarantee at all sizes.

A joint environment bootstrap (10,000 draws) fits separate intercepts and log
slopes with shared squared-log coefficient. A coverage audit withdrew the initial
three-probe 16–128 fit: rho=1/16 is absent at order 16 because its rounded
separation duplicates the earlier rho=1/32 label. No missing probe was relabelled
or imputed. The fully covered rho=1/8,1/4 subset was then added explicitly as an
exploratory sensitivity analysis; this amendment follows inspection of initial
results and is not a prospective confirmatory analysis.

| rho=1/16,1/8,1/4 | 48–128 | 0.2222 | [-7.0879, 7.1973] |

This bounded pilot neither confirms nor rules out the earlier positive signal.
Its intervals are too broad. The historical finite-environment orientation
mismatch is real; its effect on the old disorder-averaged scaling result cannot
be deduced from that mismatch alone. No theorem of equivalence or inequivalence
of the old and corrected annealed observables has been established here.

Evidence: `aztec_analysis.json`, `aztec_intervals.csv` and the saved raw pilot
under `aztec/output/corrected_reassessment_20260930/`.
