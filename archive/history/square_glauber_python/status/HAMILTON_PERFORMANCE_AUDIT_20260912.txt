Code review
-----------
The Numba local heat-bath kernel is serial: each update reads the state left
by the preceding update. Setting cpus-per-task=128 does not parallelize it.
Independent environments already run concurrently. Once most finish, the
remaining few sequential streams limit wall-clock completion.
Two independent samples per environment are currently obtained sequentially.
Splitting them into separate workers could reduce two-stream task latency by
up to roughly 2x, but needs a validated change to output ownership/aggregation.
The lower and upper CFTP bounding trajectories could also be evaluated in
parallel on the same supplied instructions; this requires explicit parallel
code, not just another Slurm option. Neither change was applied to live jobs.
A GPU allocation will not accelerate this CPU-compiled Numba kernel as-is.
Parallel face updates or active-face-only sampling would change the specified
kernel and are not acceptable shortcuts here.

The runner reconstructs full heights for order verification after each random
chunk. It does NOT reconstruct them after every face update. Larger chunks
reduce this overhead without changing assigned random streams or observables.
Verification remains at every chosen chunk boundary and every CFTP horizon.
Thus diagnostic check cadence changes explicitly, while trajectory does not.

long trajectories. A separate instrumented profile (0.739 s, instrumentation

Runtime calculation for running L=32 cases
-----------------------------------------
Face count F=(32-1)^2=961. Current cap H=268,435,456 sweeps.
Starting at horizon 1 and doubling through H replays
1+2+4+...+H = 2H-1 sweeps per bound.
Total coupled instructions per unresolved stream:
F(2H-1)=515,932,945,471.
Total individual bound updates = 1,031,865,890,942.
At measured default rate R=15,442,648 instructions/s:
t=F(2H-1)/R=9.28 hours per stream; two sequential streams ~18.56 hours.
This estimates work to reach the cap, not the unknown coalescence horizon.
Tasks 736,744,876 had one unresolved stream on entry; 831,855,889 had two.
At ~8 hours elapsed, nominal remaining times are ~1.3 and ~10.6 hours.
Allowing node/state variability, roughly 1–4 hours for one-stream tasks and
10–16 hours for two-stream tasks is a planning range, not a statistical CI.
With start 09:22 Rome Saturday, the latter suggests roughly Sunday morning;
the enforced deadline is Sunday 13 September at 21:22 Rome.

Restart decision
----------------
At 20% less total runtime, restart helps only if elapsed time is below ~20%
of original total work, assuming no reusable in-flight checkpoint. At ~8h,
these jobs have already exceeded that fraction of the estimated 9–19h work.
Therefore restarting merely to change chunk size is likely to delay completion.
No jobs were cancelled, restarted, moved, or given unused extra cores.

The five already-censored environments at L16,L20,L28 will NOT complete by
waiting for the six running jobs. Their next horizon would be 536,870,912,
requiring roughly twice the replay work at the same rate if they reach that
cap, or ~1.6x with the measured batching speedup. Exact future completion has
no defensible finite ETA without additional information about coalescence.
Small remaining height gaps are not a probabilistic guarantee of quick success.
Longer partitions avoid wall-time cancellation but do not speed up computation.

Recommendation
--------------
Let current jobs finish. Use the tested larger chunk setting for future work.
Before another expensive extension, implement and validate replay/checkpoint
reuse and/or independent-sample workers, or discuss a different exact sampling
strategy with the supervisor. Finishing the entire campaign by Monday cannot
be promised. Keep every hard environment and the strict completeness gate.
